Lab 03 — Chaotic Text Generator: HMM vs trigram test bench

App: [public Streamlit URL] · Code: [GitHub repository URL] · All numbers: 500 generated sentences, seed 42.

1. Problem and solution

A text generator has no reference answer, so its quality cannot be judged from a few examples; it must be measured on hundreds of sentences with automatic metrics. We estimate a supervised POS-HMM (start, transition and emission tables counted on 960 training sentences, 6 tags) and generate by sampling a tag sequence and then one word per tag, with a temperature (weights count1/T). We compare plain states, role-split states (S / O / P) and a trigram baseline, and tag sentences with log-space Viterbi (add-k, k = 0.01). A Streamlit test bench exposes generation, four metrics, a Viterbi tagger and a CSV export.

Trigram57.0%76.0%91.2%0.105

Why the plain HMM scores low. With first-order transitions the next tag depends only on the current tag. After a NOUN the model draws VERB (0.39), PREP (0.22) or PUNCT (0.39) whatever the role of the noun, so it ends a sentence right after the subject (“the quiet school .”) or adds a second verb after the object (“the boys visit the rice sell the young farmers .”). Why role-split reaches 100%. NOUN_S, NOUN_O and NOUN_P are different states: NOUN_S is always followed by VERB, NOUN_O by PREP or PUNCT, NOUN_P by PUNCT. The state now carries the position in the sentence, so every sampled path follows one of the 16 training patterns. (Tagging accuracy is 100% for both, including watch 37/37.)

3. Choice of temperature

  • Writing toy: role-split HMM at T = 1.5. Structure stays at 100% at every temperature while diversity rises (distinct-2 0.107 → 0.161 from T = 0.5 to 1.5), and odd sentences are acceptable or even fun in a toy.
  • Autocomplete: trigram at T = 0.5. A suggestion must be safe and predictable: at T = 0.5 the trigram has its best valid-pattern (63.6% vs 44.2% at T = 2.5) and subject-verb plausibility (79.2%); its repetitiveness and copies are not a problem for completion.

4. One good and one bad sentence

  • Good (role-split, T = 1.0): “the children eat the bread at the park .” — valid pattern, plausible pair, novel.
  • Bad (role-split, T = 1.0): “the foxes lose by the school .” — failure type meaning: the pattern DET NOUN VERB PREP DET NOUN PUNCT is valid, but the pair (foxes, lose) never occurs. Cause: the verb is emitted from the VERB state only, independently of the subject noun, so states give grammar but not meaning.
  • Other failure types seen: structure — “the happy market .” (plain HMM, invalid pattern DET ADJ NOUN PUNCT); memorisation — “the girls leave the park .” (trigram, T = 0.5, copied verbatim from training: not creative).

5. What state splitting changed, and what it did not

Splitting NOUN, ADJ and DET by role gave the first-order model the memory it lacked: the valid tag pattern rose from 16.2% to 100% at every temperature, the model still generates new sentences (99.4% novel) and Viterbi tagging stays at 100%. It did not give the model meaning: words are still emitted independently given the state, so subject-verb plausibility is only 60.6%, below the trigram (76.0%), which sees the actual previous words. Diversity also drops (distinct-2 0.198 → 0.148) because the paths are more constrained. This is a negative result worth reporting: 100% structure is not 100% quality.

6. Real-data test (UD English EWT, 12,544 train / 2,077 test sentences, 17 tags)

The synthetic grammar has 6 tags, a small closed vocabulary, 16 tag sequences and an ambiguity (watch) that the previous tag always resolves, so 100% is expected and says nothing about real English. On real web text the same code gains only 1.9 points over the most-frequent-tag baseline and gets 40.4% of whole sentences right. 7.5% of test tokens are unknown and are tagged at only 28.2%: the smoothed emission k / (count(tag) + k·V) is largest for rare tags, so unknown words drift to X and INTJ (PROPN→X 195, PROPN→INTJ 137). With 10,359 distinct tag sequences for 12,544 sentences, the valid-tag-pattern metric is meaningless on real text. A first-order HMM without word-shape or suffix features is far from a state-of-the-art tagger.
